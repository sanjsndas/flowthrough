    • About Flow Through Space

    An independent journal for people who believe that the spaces we inhabit shape the lives we lead.

    Our Mission

    Flow Through Space began in 2018 as a personal notebook—a place to collect ideas about how interiors and architecture can be more human, more sustainable, and more quietly beautiful. That notebook became a journal, and the journal became a community of designers, architects, and curious homeowners.

    We publish long-form essays, practical guides, and honest material explorations. We are not interested in trends for their own sake, nor in decoration as a form of consumption. We are interested in how space feels: the weight of a door, the temperature of light at dusk, the way a hallway can calm you down before you even reach the living room.

    Every article is written by our small editorial team, fact-checked, and reviewed by practising architects and designers. We take no sponsored content. No advertorials. Just independent, thoughtful writing.

    Editorial Philosophy

    We believe that good design is not about what you add, but what you remove. Restraint, honesty in materials, and respect for natural light are the values that run through every article we publish.

    We also believe that design writing should be accessible. No jargon, no gatekeeping. Whether you're an architect or someone renovating their first apartment, you should be able to read our work and come away with something useful.

    Finally, we believe in slowness. We publish two to three articles per week—not fifty. Each piece is given the time it deserves. In a world of endless content, we choose depth over volume.

    EM

    Elena Marchetti

    Editor & Founder

    Elena is an architect and design writer based in Milan. She trained at Politecnico di Milano and spent a decade working on residential and adaptive-reuse projects before founding Flow Through Space in 2018.

    Her writing has appeared in Domus, Dezeen, and The Architectural Review. She is a passionate advocate for low-carbon materials, slow interiors, and the idea that a well-designed home is a form of quiet activism.

    When she's not editing or writing, she can be found sketching in cafés, restoring her 1930s apartment, or teaching a masterclass on materiality at the Milan campus of POLI.design.
